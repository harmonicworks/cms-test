## Second hand gear

I am a big fan of second hand gear. Cassette machines, old music equipment, fun toy instruments, all that good stuff.

Ok, so, vintage gear has some awesome feel to it, but more on that later.
So, feel free to browse around on my site, see what sticks and simply have fun exploring!
Reason for sharing all this.
There is a reason why I wanted to create this blog.
As I am a fan of second hand gear, from old tape machines to old music gear, I like to scout thrift stores and second hand sites.
Thing sthat attract me are usually the odd ones. the insanly cheap versions of valuable gear, or the old ones that where expensive then but ar echeap now. Outdated technology but still usable, basically.
But, here it also starts. Still usable would be, uhm, for me. I think it is usable, but others might disagree. Which is fine ofcourse :-)
A good example of this would be the Sony mavica FD7. A diskette based camera, which puts out a whopping amount of about 40 images on one diskette, in the high definition of .6 megapixels!
If those numbers don't say anything, the images are below the size of a generic profile image on any social media page.....
Or, the size of a postal stamp....
Awesome! That is the thing that I like a lot! It is usable, but has its quirks, but another cool thing is, it has its limits. Hard limits, not made limits.
Those limits steer my creativity. I have to work within those limits, and find ways to get something cool out of it. With cameras, music gear and old audio devices, I think thatis awesome.
This comes with a price tho. Not only will that create some bridges between old and current technology, like getting the actual files off the device, or simply connecting it in a meaningfull way to make use of it, it also needs maintanance of even repair.
Figuring out how you can get all this sorted out is part of the process for me. And, along the way, I might stumble upon fun surprising details, but also manage to get it running again, or get it connected.
In some cases, that journey needs to be shared. For both the fun, but also a guidaance for others who want to try out the same device, but might be stuck here and there.
Sharing is caring, right?

## The graspable ceiling

after working with music projects, sound design, old gear, all that fun stuff, I noticed a pattern in what I was searching for. I like devices that have a "graspable ceiling". I like to know what the possibilities could be on a device. Not to trying out to got to that ceiling with all of them, but almost seeing those max settings, or limited options makes the device understandable in a way. On the other end, a device that does a lot of things under the hood can be fine. but there is a certain level of control you seem to be giving up, at least that is how it feels to me.
good examples are, the sony mavica fd7 for images, being as basic digitallyas you can get them. or a alphasmart neo for simple text or book writing. even a brother whatchamacallit version with backlight often works better than a full laptop with all bells and whistles. not that a laptop is bad, or a brother typewriter is the best, it feels like it serves a purpose and nothign else. one could argue that it is a bit indicative of this aera, where groups of people take a step back from over automation and want seperate devices for seperate things. You could do many things on your phone, like gaming, writing, recording, photos, book reading and listening to music. but using dedicated devices instead makes the experience more unique. again, personal taste and opinion, haha.
Usisng a small handheld gaming device, or an ereader, or neo/alphasmart or portable audio player detaches you from other distractions that could interfere with the process you are actually decided to do in the first place.
with audio/music gear, I feel the same thing. the less the device can do, the more focussed you could become on the thing it CAN do. a casio cz101 cannot sample, or create high quality pcm sounds that morph, including onboard dsp effects. It can do phase distortion, that is it. a roland vs880 can record multiple tracks, and a su10 can only sample 12 bit stuff.....for a few seconds....
Those limits feel great to me. you basically know what you are getting right from the start. on the opposite end, at least for me, live the do all devices, the latest being the small fm synth for under 100 euros. impressive for what it all can do, but for me, what CAN it do exactly. Why should I buy it? If you want to have it all, this is fine, but it feels like a toolbox for multiple expertieses, from gardner to woodcutter to electrician. To each its own, but not for me.

That is where most of my gear comes from. Stumbling upon odd gear, broken, or simply not used for 20+ years, or interesting enough to tinker with.

## From gear to tools

many tools come in many forms. some are quite usable right out of the box, like a mavica or an alphasmart neo. others, even tho they are not overly difficult to use, offer their set pallete of tools in a nice box, but sometimes have limited access to those tools. the vs880 is a good exmaple of that. fairly easy to use, as it has many buttons on the device already, but it also has some extra settings that need menudiving, thus not always as intuitive as you might want. trying out if there might be software options to "open it up" a bit are not only interesting, they also make it able to do things that are not really mentioned in a guide or manual. for the vs880 tool, this meant that I cna now use the software to mapout certain settings on the device together with my korg nanokontrol! want more reverb during a live recording? go ahead, twiddle the slider to make it so.
another good example might be the sy22. not an overly difficult device in itself, compared to sibings of the yamaha series, but again, menu diving is needed to reach the parts of the device that could be tweaked. fun detail on this machine, it has extra settings that are not accessable on the device itself, but only via the midi tool.
So, in essence, it makes working with these tools a bit easier, and even opens up new possibilites!

During that journey of figuring out what the heck the device is even capable off, i scout the internet to search for sources. sometimes i find many, but sometimes none, or confusing ones.
old devices are a mixed bag in terms of sharing information themselfs. for some, you connect them and instantly see what they are capable off. or they have detailed manuals online that "open them up" so to speak. With others, not so much. No manuals, or at least, extreme short ones, and mixed experiences on fora tend to lead to a healthy dose of scrambled information. Sometimes experiences differ so much, that conclusions drift to all sides.

## What this blog is, and what it isn't

Now, what the blog is and what it isn't.
As the stories on this blog are basically my journey in tinkering, they are by no means definitive guides, or maintanance/repair guides. Please check your gear and proper guides yourself. all tinkering is done on my own gear, and if something broke, it was on me. I am not responsible for your gear, so try all with caution. that being said, as I do nt want to break my gear obviously, i tried to include as much detail and information to make sure the process works in the bst way. I will include errors I made or odd bumps in the road, but the choice of trying the same hacks or repairs on your own gear is fully yours (the readers).
what it is...is simply a notebook of projects that where or where not successfull. whichever the outcome, if the journey opened up new ways, who knows who can continue those.

what it also isnt. I disabled comments. not that I dont want to hear your voice but more to make this more static. as I see it as a notebook, feel free to read it whenevre you like. no need for anything else.
also, the tools are shared as is. in many cases they work, but they are free to re-use and re-work with if you want. they are on github, opensource, and free to use and download. for that reason, I will not schedule updates. additions and features get added while working with the projects myself. this full process is not clear at the moment of writing (october 2026) and might change in the future. for now, it simply is what it is :-)

A small note before you dive in: everything on this site worked on _my_ devices, with my cables, firmware, settings and software. Your setup might be different, and so might your results. Nothing here is "this is how it should be done, period". It's "this is what worked for me, with these devices, these settings and these tools". Nothing is hidden: the tools are open source (MIT) and listed in each post. Back up first, take your time, and enjoy finding your own way with it.

Enjoy your stay on this blog, and I hope the tools and information shared is of some value to you!
